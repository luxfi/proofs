\subsection*{Pulsar DKG soundness}

\begin{theorem}[Pulsar DKG soundness]
\label{thm:pulsar-dkg-sound}
For every $\PPT$ adversary $\mathcal{A}$ against the DKG-SOUND game
of \texttt{pulsar/spec/security-games.tex},
\[
  \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{DKG\text{-}SOUND}}_{\mathsf{Pulsar\text{-}M}}(\mathcal{A})
  \;\le\;
  \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{MSIS}}_{q, k, 2\ell, \beta_{\mathrm{bind}}}(\mathcal{B})
  \;+\;
  \frac{q_H}{2^{256}}.
\]
Concretely: a successful DKG-SOUND adversary either breaks the
Pedersen-commit binding (an MSIS instance over $[\mathbf{A} | \mathbf{B}]$)
or finds a $\cSHAKE256$ pre-image (an RO term bounded by $q_H /
2^{256}$). Both terms are negligible at every Pulsar parameter
level.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Honest parties verify Pedersen identities at Round~2
(\Cref{sec:dkg}). A corrupted dealer that contributes a malformed
share fails the $A \cdot f_i(j) + B \cdot g_i(j) \stackrel{?}{=}
\sum_k j^k C_{i,k}$ check; failing this check yields a
\textsc{ComplaintBadDelivery} carrying the (share, blind, commits)
tuple as evidence, which is publicly verifiable by any third party.
The Pedersen binding bound prevents the adversary from substituting
a different $\mathbf{c}_{i,0}$ between commit and reveal -- a binding
violation is by construction an MSIS solution over $[\mathbf{A} |
\mathbf{B}]$.

The remaining $q_H / 2^{256}$ term bounds the probability of a
$\cSHAKE256$ commit-digest collision at the Round-1.5 equivocation
gate: a successful equivocation requires the adversary to produce
two distinct commit vectors with the same 256-bit digest, which
is by construction a collision in the random oracle. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Identifiable abort.}
By construction every failure mode in the DKG produces a signed
\textsc{Complaint} record naming the deviator
(\Cref{sec:abort}):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0pt]
\item Equivocation $\Rightarrow$ \textsc{ComplaintEquivocation} (two
distinct commit broadcasts from one sender);
\item Bad delivery $\Rightarrow$ \textsc{ComplaintBadDelivery} (a
private share that fails the Pedersen identity check at the
recipient).
\end{itemize}

Both complaints are publicly verifiable from the transcript alone
(the Pedersen check is determined by the public $(\mathbf{A},
\mathbf{B})$). The chain governance layer consumes the complaints
and slashes the disqualified party.

\subsection*{Pulsar DKG share privacy}

\begin{theorem}[Pulsar DKG share privacy]
\label{thm:pulsar-dkg-priv}
For every $\PPT$ adversary $\mathcal{A}$ against the DKG-PRIV game
of \texttt{pulsar/spec/security-games.tex} with up to $t-1$
corrupted parties,
\[
  \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{DKG\text{-}PRIV}}_{\mathsf{Pulsar\text{-}M}}(\mathcal{A})
  \;\le\;
  \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{MLWE}}_{q, k, \ell, \eta}(\mathcal{B}).
\]
The reduction loses a constant factor independent of the committee
size $n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pedersen commitments are computationally hiding under M-LWE on
$\mathbf{B}$ (\Cref{thm:pedersen-hiding}). The simulator chooses
corrupted-party shares uniformly from the simulation distribution
induced by $\mathit{pk}$, then programs the commits via the
M-LWE-based hiding simulator. Distinguishing real from simulated
commits reduces to M-LWE in $\mathbf{B}$.

The corrupted-party share leakage is information-theoretically zero
for $|\mathcal{C}| \le t - 1$ by Shamir secret sharing over any
field of order $> n$. The Pulsar reference implementation uses
GF($257$) for $n \le 256$ and GF($q$) for $n \le q - 1$; both
satisfy the field-order condition for every committee size in
scope. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scaling.}
\Cref{thm:pulsar-dkg-priv}'s bound is independent of the
committee size $n$. The DKG share-privacy guarantee therefore
extends verbatim to the extreme committee size $N^\star =
1\,111\,111$.
