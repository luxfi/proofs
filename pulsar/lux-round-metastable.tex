\subsection*{Lux round metastable signing}

This file pins the composition theorem for the canonical Lux
deployment at validator pool sizes $N > 256$: Pulsar (3, 2) at the
per-Lux-round committee level, driven by the Lux Wave protocol's
K-sample, with beta-confidence amplification and a P3Q STARK rollup.

\paragraph{Lux primitives.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Prism.Cut}
(\texttt{consensus/protocol/prism/cut.go}). Stake-weighted or
uniform $K$-sampler over the validator pool.
\item \textbf{Wave} (\texttt{consensus/protocol/wave/wave.go}).
Threshold voting primitive driven by Prism.Cut. Each Wave Tick is
one Lux round.
\item \textbf{Focus} (\texttt{consensus/protocol/focus/focus.go}).
Beta-consecutive-success tracker. After $\beta$ successful Lux
rounds with alpha-of-K agreement on the same item, Focus fires
"decided".
\item \textbf{Photon} (\texttt{consensus/protocol/wave/wave.go:14}).
The per-round wire message Wave validators emit. Carries the
validator's preference vote and -- in the metastable-signing
deployment -- a Pulsar Round-1 commit on the preferred item.
\item \textbf{Quasar} (\texttt{consensus/protocol/quasar/}).
The composite signing layer that runs BLS + Pulsar + ML-DSA
under one cert envelope.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Lux round.}
A \emph{Lux round} is one Wave Tick:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0pt]
\item Prism.Cut samples $K$ validators from the pool ($K = 21$ is
the default Wave config; $K = 3$ is the minimum for $(3, 2)$
Pulsar).
\item Each sampled validator emits a Photon carrying its current
preference $\hat v$ on the item under vote, plus a Pulsar
Round-1 commit $D_i = \cSHAKE(\bar{\mathbf{w}}_i\,\|\,\tau_1)$ where
$\tau_1$ binds the Lux round context $(\mathit{epoch}, \mathit{round},
\mathit{item}, \mathit{committee root})$.
\item The aggregator collects Photons. If $\ge \alpha$ of the $K$
agree on $\hat v$, the round is \emph{successful} and Pulsar
Round-2 reveals are collected from those $\alpha$ validators.
\item Focus increments its counter for $\hat v$ by $1$. If the
counter reaches $\beta$, Wave fires "decided" -- the item is
finalised.
\end{enumerate}

The Pulsar Round-1 commits and Round-2 reveals from successful
Lux rounds accumulate at the Quasar layer. After $\beta$ successful
rounds, the accumulator has $\beta \cdot \alpha$ Pulsar sig
contributions across distinct $K$-samples. The P3Q rollup attests
that each per-round Pulsar (3, 2) signature verifies under
unmodified FIPS~204 \texttt{ML-DSA.Verify}.

\paragraph{Per-Lux-round binding.}
The per-round Pulsar session ID is derived deterministically from
the Lux round context:
\[
  \mathit{sid}_r \;=\; \cSHAKE(\text{Encode}(\mathit{ctx}_r), 16, \text{`PULSAR-SIGN-PRNG-V1'}).
\]
Each Wave Tick increments $\mathit{round}$, producing a fresh
$\mathit{sid}_r$ and forcing the per-round PRNG to re-seed. This
closes the CRIT-1 cross-round replay vector from del Pino-Niot.

\begin{theorem}[Lux round metastable composition]
\label{thm:lux-round-metastable}
For every $\PPT$ adversary $\mathcal{A}$ corrupting at most a
$\rho$-fraction of the validator pool, the probability that
$\mathcal{A}$ forges a Lux-round-finalised certificate on a message
$\mu_*$ not honestly signed is bounded by
\[
  \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{cert\text{-}forge}}_{\mathsf{Lux}}(\mathcal{A})
  \;\le\;
  \tau(K, \alpha, \rho)^\beta
  \cdot \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{TS\text{-}UF}}_{\mathsf{Pulsar\text{-}M}\text{-}L}(\mathcal{B}_1)
  \;+\;
  \beta \cdot \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{soundness}}_{\mathsf{P3Q}}(\mathcal{B}_2)
  \;+\;
  \beta \cdot \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{beacon\text{-}unbias}}_{\mathsf{Quasar}}(\mathcal{B}_3),
\]
where $\tau(K, \alpha, \rho) = \Pr[X \ge \alpha \mid X \sim
\operatorname{Bin}(K, \rho)]$ is the per-Lux-round adversary
agreement probability.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Hybrid sequence:

\textbf{H0} -- real game. $\mathcal{A}$ outputs a forged cert.

\textbf{H1} -- replace P3Q by direct per-round
\texttt{ML-DSA.Verify} on each accumulated Pulsar signature.
Distinguishing advantage is bounded by
$\beta \cdot \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{soundness}}_{\mathsf{P3Q}}$ (one
per accumulated round).

\textbf{H2} -- replace Wave's Prism.Cut sample by uniform random
$K$-subsets. Beacon-unbiasability bounds the distinguishing advantage
by $\beta \cdot \mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{beacon\text{-}unbias}}_{\mathsf{Quasar}}$.

\textbf{H3} -- the probability that $\mathcal{A}$ controls at least
$\alpha$ of $K$ in a uniformly random sample from a pool with
$\rho$-fraction corruptions is exactly $\tau(K, \alpha, \rho)$ (the
binomial tail). Over $\beta$ rounds, the probability of agreement
in every round is at most $\tau(K, \alpha, \rho)^\beta$.

\textbf{H4} -- conditioned on the adversary holding at most
$\alpha - 1$ in every round, any per-round Pulsar forgery
contradicts the $(K, \alpha-1)$-corruption-bounded TS-UF of
Pulsar at level $L$ (\Cref{thm:pulsar-tsuf}). Per-round
advantage is bounded by
$\mathrm{Adv}^{\mathsf{TS\text{-}UF}}_{\mathsf{Pulsar\text{-}M}\text{-}L}$.

Summing the hybrid bounds gives the theorem. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical instantiation at $N = 1\,111\,111$, $K = 21$,
$\alpha = 15$, $\beta = 12$.}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0pt]
\item At $\rho = 1/3$ (standard Byzantine bound),
$\tau(21, 15, 1/3) \approx 2^{-11.3}$ (reference implementation
\texttt{ApproxRoundSecurity} confirms numerically).
\item Beta-amplified: $\tau^{\beta} = 2^{-11.3 \cdot 12} \approx 2^{-135}$.
\item Per-round Pulsar TS-UF advantage (level 65, NIST PQ
category 3): $\le 2^{-180}$.
\item P3Q soundness: $\le 2^{-100}$.
\item Beacon unbiasability: $\le 2^{-128}$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Total cert-forge advantage at $K = 21$, $\alpha = 15$,
$\beta = 12$:
$\le 2^{-135} \cdot 2^{-180} + 12 \cdot 2^{-100} + 12 \cdot 2^{-128}
\approx 2^{-96}$. The dominant term is the P3Q soundness; the
metastable layer contributes a $2^{-315}$ tail that is essentially
free.

For the $(K, \alpha) = (3, 2)$ minimum-cost profile at $\rho = 1/3$:
$\tau(3, 2, 1/3) \approx 0.26$ per round; $\beta = 60$ rounds give
$\tau^\beta \approx 2^{-117}$. The $(3, 2) \times \beta = 60$ profile
trades wire bandwidth (3 sigs per round) for round count.

\paragraph{Per-round cost.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0pt]
\item Compute: $K \times$ (Pulsar Round-1 commit $\sim 0.5$~ms +
Round-2 reveal $\sim 0.5$~ms) $\approx 21$~ms at $K = 21$.
\item Wire: $K$ Photons each carrying one 32-byte commit + one
MAC bundle $\approx 1$~KB per Photon = 21~KB / round.
\item Storage: one Pulsar share + per-round PRNG state $\approx$ 4~KB
per validator across the epoch.
\end{itemize}

Total per-block ($\beta$ rounds): $\sim 250$~KB wire, $\sim 250$~ms
compute, single $\sim$700-byte P3Q proof emitted at finality.

\paragraph{Liveness piggy-backs on consensus.}
The Lux round metastable signing inherits Wave's liveness:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0pt]
\item If Wave terminates (Focus reaches $\beta$ on some item),
the metastable signature exists by construction.
\item If Wave does not terminate, the metastable signature does not
either -- which is the correct behaviour: the consensus has not yet
decided what to sign.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Adaptive corruption resistance.}
Because each Lux round samples a fresh $K$-committee from
Prism.Cut, the adversary must control a fresh $\alpha$-of-$K$
sample every round to maintain a per-round bias. This is the same
"moving target" property that the Lux Wave protocol exploits for
consensus safety, repurposed here for threshold signing. The
binomial-tail bound $\tau(K, \alpha, \rho)^\beta$ captures this
amplification.
